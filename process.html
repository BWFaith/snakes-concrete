How we work

A clear process from first call to final walk-through, so you always know what happens next.

Four steps, no surprises

  1. Free estimate

    Share your plans by phone, WhatsApp or the booking form. We visit, measure, check access and slope, and discuss finish options.

  2. Prep and forming

    We grade, compact the gravel base, set forms, and place wire mesh or rebar. Plumbing and electrical sleeves are checked first.

  3. Pour and finish

    The truck arrives, the crew places and screeds the concrete, then floats, finishes and cuts joints at the right time.

  4. Cure and walk-through

    We cover care instructions, remove forms, clean up and walk the finished job with you.

Before pour day

A little preparation on your side keeps the day on schedule.

  • Mark underground utilitiesCall 811 before any digging. It is free and required in most places.
  • Clear the work areaMove vehicles, planters, furniture and anything near the pour.
  • Make sure trucks can reach the siteTell us about gates, tight turns, overhead wires or soft ground.
  • Plan around the cure timeKeep foot, pet and vehicle traffic off until we say it is safe.

Cure and care timeline

Concrete keeps getting stronger for weeks. Here is a general guide.

When What you can do
Day 1 to 2 Keep everyone and everything off. Mind pets and children.
After 24 to 48 hours Light foot traffic is usually fine.
About 7 days Passenger vehicles can usually return to driveways and garage floors.
28 days Concrete reaches its design strength. Heavy loads, de-icing salts and sealing wait until now.

We give you a tailored schedule for your mix and weather at the walk-through.

Weather and scheduling

Concrete is picky about temperature and rain. We watch the forecast and talk to you early if a day is not right for your pour.

Hot weather speeds curing, so we plan the pour and finishing carefully. Cold weather slows it and needs protection from frost. Heavy rain can damage a fresh surface.

How long before I can walk or drive on new concrete?
Light foot traffic is usually fine after 24 to 48 hours. Keep vehicles off for about 7 days, and heavy loads for 28 days when concrete reaches full design strength.
How thick should a driveway be?
Four inches is standard for cars. Five or six inches is better for trucks, trailers and heavy equipment. We recommend a thickness for your use when we quote.
Will my concrete crack?
All concrete can crack as it cures and shrinks. We place control joints so any cracking happens along the saw-cut lines, and we prepare the base to reduce settling.
Do you pour in hot or cold weather?
Yes, with the right steps. Cold pours need protection from freezing, and hot pours need extra attention to mix and curing. We will move a date if conditions are not safe for the concrete.
